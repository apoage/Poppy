## The audit (independent re-derivation from model.py, not the summary)

Ran an independent auditor over `runs/810_postcost_headroom/model.py`. Mechanical gates: **sound** —
no-leak CONFIRMED (the model never sees the realized return; entry-lagged t+1; causal features),
apples-to-apples CONFIRMED (same portfolio/cost/eval, only the scorer differs), value-identity CONFIRMED
(feature assert aborts on drift; ridge IC 0.05982 == 806's 0.0598). But it caught **two real problems**:

1. **A Sharpe-estimator inconsistency (my bug).** The result-table windows used a PER-BAR row grid
   (`np.arange(lo,hi+1)`, n=217419, overlapping 20-bar holds) yet annualized with the ÷H per-rebalance
   factor — an internally inconsistent estimator, the same FAMILY as the 809 per-bar trap this task was
   meant to avoid. Symmetric across arms (so it didn't flip the ordering) but the magnitudes/CIs weren't
   what the docstring claimed. (The val eval during training WAS correctly H-strided; only the verdict
   windows drifted to per-bar.)
2. **The crux was undetermined without a gross measurement.** Both arms negative; the objective EXPLICITLY
   rewards turnover reduction → "beats linear" was consistent with being ALL cost-saving (turnover 0.94 vs
   1.36), which is cost-floor-bound and wouldn't extrapolate. No cost=0 Sharpe recorded to distinguish a
   genuine scoring edge from pure turnover reduction.

## The fix + the decisive re-run

Fixed both: eval rows → **H-strided non-overlapping decisions** (per-rebalance, matching the H-strided
training); added the **GROSS (cost=0) Sharpe** alongside net for model and linear. Re-ran (VRAM-resident,
GPU2, ~4 min). The corrected **TEST_oos** (n=10871 decisions):

| | net Sharpe | **gross (cost=0)** | turnover |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **model** | −100.83 | **+15.43** | 0.89 |
| linear | −274.47 | **+24.43** | 1.39 |

**net-beat = True, GROSS-beat = FALSE.** In every in-sample regime too, linear's gross scoring beats the
model's (model gross −8…+26 vs linear +88…+169).

## Verdict: TURNOVER ARTIFACT — do NOT greenlight 811. **Fork-C closes.**

The model scores **worse gross than plain linear** (+15 vs +24). Its only "win" was trading less (0.89 vs
1.39 → less cost drag → less-negative net). The post-cost Sharpe objective did exactly as told — and on a
cost-bound signal the cheapest way to raise post-cost Sharpe is **trade less**, not score better. It
dampened positions, cutting turnover AND gross edge together.

**Why 811 (a bigger model) won't help — the structural reason:** the edge and the turnover are COUPLED —
the edge IS in the trading. You cannot cut cost without cutting the edge; the model proved it (lower
turnover dropped gross proportionally). Gross is positive (+15/+24 — the 806 signal is real, IC 0.06 at
H=20), but at the turnover needed to capture it, 21bp taker > edge. Same ceiling 808 and 809 found for
linear, now confirmed for models. A bigger model on the same objective inherits the same trap.

## What this closes

This is the **Fork-C the campaign pre-authorized**: the 1-min Kraken signal is REAL but **cost-floor-bound
— for linear AND for a right-objective model.** 806→810 have now exhausted the modeling surface:
- 806: signal exists (clean-OOS IC 0.06–0.15).

**The lever was never more modeling — it's a data/problem decision:** a cheaper cost regime (809 showed
maker marginal at best), a different venue/instrument, or accept the ceiling and harvest the methodology.
That's the operator's call.
