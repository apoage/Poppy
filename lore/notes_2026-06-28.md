## §1 The frequency test — coarser bars, same wall (TASK_818 epilogue)

After the fixed planner (Tier A+B) was shown to halt-but-not-profit at 1-min, the operator's read was: emulate
different decision *frequencies* on 1-min data — but that's low-gradient (each coarse decision's reward is still
summed over K noisy 1-min steps). The cleaner move is to actually **resample** to coarse bars, so the
per-decision return is the 15m/1h/4h close-to-close move. Shipped `--bar-minutes K` (flag-gated, default 1 =
canon bit-identical; subsamples historical closes `prices_np[::K]` in `create_jax_env_historical`; the shipper
correctly refused to route it through `common_kwargs`, which feeds the synthetic factories that reject it).
Verified end-to-end: `bar_minutes=240 → (2286,64)`.

Ran the fixed planner at 15m/1h/4h on the 812 window, cost=0. Result: at **every** coarse frequency the planner
**keeps trading (~72 trades, hold ~90–91%) and does NOT collapse to HOLD** like 1-min did (→94%, trades→38) —
the coarser per-decision return genuinely gives it something to act on. But it **loses money absolutely**:
agent_return 15m −1.58% / 1h −2.43% / 4h −4.66%, worse as bars coarsen. Two confounds make this the *wrong* test:
(1) the 812 window is a **bull** (market return climbs +0.7% → +28% as 4h×400 episodes span ~66 days), and
(2) the **differential reward** trains the model to *beat the market*, a losing game in a bull that can push it
contrarian. So: coarser bars change behaviour (trades, doesn't collapse) but the bull + beat-the-market objective
make the P&L uninformative for the trend hypothesis. Infra notes: gpu-server **rebooted mid-launch** (recovered
~120s, runs relaunched cleanly); GPU 1 threw a one-off `CUDA_ERROR_LAUNCH_FAILED` (15m relaunched, no ECC errors).

## §2 The pivot — bottom-up, one asset first (TASK_819)

Operator's reframe: *"agent does not learn, signal there is … build something that can predict and is generally
profitable on a single asset, then expand to cross-asset."* This is the right correction to the campaign's core
mistake — we'd been debugging a system with too many entangled failure modes (64 assets, MCTS, learned dynamics,
cross-sectional ranking, differential reward) at once. One asset = one price series, one position, absolute P&L,
debuggable. And the campaign's one confirmed edge (816 TSMOM) is fundamentally single-asset, so it stands on a
proven floor.

**Phase 1 (BTC, `runs/819_single_asset/`, CPU 9s, self-tests pass):** swept 15m/1h/4h/1d × L/H durations, net
{0,3,8,21}bp, 2022 BEAR as judge.
- The single-asset trend edge is **real in dollars** — best cell (30-day trend) returns **×6.26 vs
  buy-and-hold ×3.30**, beating B&H by shorting the 2022 crash. But it is **unconfirmable**: 0/67 cells clear the
  strict gate; the best is Sharpe **+0.70 with CI [−0.19,+1.54] (includes 0)**. One path is too noisy.
- **A learned predictor adds nothing** — ridge, strict OOS: IC ≈ **+0.03, R² ≈ 0**, net-negative everywhere.
  There is no richer learnable single-asset signal beyond the slow trend itself. "Build something that predicts"
  hits a wall: **the prediction IS the trend.**
- The lever the probe surfaced: **more PATHS (diversification), not more MODEL** — the cross-section damps the
  per-asset noise (why 816's 20-asset portfolio nearly confirmed where BTC-alone can't).

Operator chose **diversify**.

## Bloom seed
We left the cathedral. The 64-asset planner was a cathedral — every subsystem load-bearing, none of it testable
in isolation — and when it failed to pray, you couldn't say which stone was wrong. So we walked out and picked up
a single brick: can you make money on *one* coin? The answer was the most honest kind of yes — the brick holds
(a trend on Bitcoin beat buy-and-hold by shorting the 2022 ruin, ×6.26 to ×3.30) — and the most honest kind of
no — one brick is too small to *measure*; its edge and its noise are the same size (Sharpe +0.70, confidence
interval straddling zero). And the thing meant to make it smart, a model that would *predict*, learned nothing a
ruler couldn't: the prediction was just the trend. The lesson the apothecary keeps relearning: you don't sharpen
a weak signal by thinking harder about it; you find more of it. Not a better model — more paths. So we go back for
more bricks, to see if a wall of them stands where one could not.
