## §107 — TASK_738 legacy_27ch: clear regression (closes TASK_732 question)

Reverting cross-sectional encoder input (drop channels 27-38, use legacy
27-ch P1 ckpt) trains a "better classifier" (Bal.Acc +7.48pp, loss −0.98)
but trades **catastrophically worse** (Δα −18.44pp, all 3 seeds, all 6
periods negative). Bear regime worst hit (−30.09pp).

Mechanism (sample, bull period, seed 7): trade count 136 → 13,246
(50× over-trading), Sharpe +8.68 → −27.20. Same signature as
TASK_741_mint_xs.

**Conclusion**: TASK_732 cross-sectional channels (27-38) ARE load-bearing
for Alpha — the production encoder needs them. The 27-ch P1 ckpt is now
demonstrably a worse base than 39-ch. Don't revisit unless we redesign
something fundamental about how those channels are consumed.

## §108 — TASK_739 dense_sign: inconclusive, dir_acc ceiling NOT broken

Mean Δα = −2.63pp, just over the −2pp threshold. Spread is the problem:
s7 = −11.46pp, s13/s23 = +1.69/+1.88pp. dir_acc 0.569 across all 3 —
**identical to the 0.574 baseline ceiling**. The "break the dir_acc
ceiling" hypothesis (TASK_733) is **closed regardless of any 5-seed
extension** — dense_sign doesn't even move dir_acc.

Trade-rate analysis confirms: s7 baseline traded only 136 times in bull;
dense_sign made it trade 5,076 times → over-trading collapse, same
mechanism as 738/741. s13/s23 already traded heavily at baseline so dense
sign didn't trigger the same collapse.

Training loss essentially unchanged (Δ −0.025 average). Unlike 738/741,
NOT a "loss DOWN, Alpha DOWN" pattern — closer to "noise."

**Recommendation**: queue seeds 31+42 (5-seed extension) ONLY if cheap;
likely lands flat/negative. The dir_acc hypothesis is dead either way.

## §109 — TASK_734 flow_matching: PROMISING

Mean Δα = +3.38pp, **STD 3.95pp** (tightest variance of any variant
tested this stack), per-period Δα **positive in all 6 regimes**
(+0.89 to +4.89pp), no regime damage. The strict promotion rule
("mean > +3pp AND all 3 positive") fails only because s7 = −0.54pp (flat).

Mechanism is different from over-trading: trade counts move TOWARD
selectivity (s13 −76%, s23 −79%), not away from it. Identity-collapse
symptom (state_loss → 0) does NOT appear — the escalation to α-v2
(prediction head + NeuralMarketODE) per SPEC isn't triggered.

Interestingly: flow_matching helps Alpha **without** moving dir_acc
(stays 0.568–0.569). The dynamics loss improves planning, not
classification. This is exactly the kind of "Alpha-side fix" we want
post-TASK_741, since training-side metrics are unreliable on this setup.

**This is the first credible Tier-2 candidate.** Queue 5-seed extension
(seeds 31, 42) on top of existing 3. If mean +3pp holds with no clearly
negative seed, **promote `--flow-matching` to canonical Stage-B default**.

## §110 — Pattern reinforced

Post-TASK_741 success-metric rule re-validated by 738 + 739:

> Better classifier (loss DOWN, Bal.Acc UP) → worse trader (Δα DOWN, all
> seeds negative, over-trading collapse).

Trio of regressions (737 no_aux_solo single-seed outlier, 741 mint_xs,
738 legacy_27ch) all share signature: training loss improves significantly
while Alpha collapses with trade-rate explosion. Three independent
experimental setups, three confirmations — this is the dominant failure
mode of "make the encoder/classifier sharper" interventions on this stack.

**The bottleneck is downstream of the encoder.** Anything that improves
"how well does the model predict the next 60 steps" will keep failing
this way until the policy-side aggressiveness scaling is addressed.

Flow-matching is the existence proof that an "Alpha-side" mechanism can
work without touching dir_acc. Worth investigating *what kind* of
mechanism improves planning rather than classification.
