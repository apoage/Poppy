## §179 — The verdict we almost recorded

The v1 candle-language panel finished on gpu-server overnight. Five members — L1 BPE-delta,
L2 ABBA, L3 SAX-grammar, L4 FSQ-VQ, L5 Matrix-Profile — each judged by the shared non-predictive
battery, with **A8 cross-asset transfer the designated headline**. Every member that reached A8
reported the same thing: `beats_control = False`. L4 didn't even reach A8 — it crashed on a class-body
scope bug, and separately collapsed its codebook to 172 of 12,800 entries.

Read naively, that is a clean kill: a vocabulary of candle shapes fit on nine assets does not transfer
to held-out assets any better than a structure-destroyed control, across four independent tokenization
philosophies. Branch C — the active structural bet alongside Poppy's ExIt revival — looked dead.

The one detail that stopped the kill: **every member's A8 coverage was ~0.9996, and so was every
control's.** Real and null, statistically identical, on every member. That is not the signature of a
real negative. It is the signature of a dead instrument — a thermometer reading room temperature no
matter what you dip it in.

## §180 — The autopsy (workflow `wheo5htsj`, 29 agents, 17 CONFIRM / 5 DEMOTE)

I ran a six-way diagnostic fan-out: four code auditors (the A8 gate, the A1/A2 substrate, the L4 FSQ
collapse, the four symbolic members) and two literature scouts (how cross-asset tokenizer transfer is
*properly* measured; how to fix FSQ underutilization), each finding adversarially cross-validated. The
verdict was decisive and, in hindsight, embarrassing in the productive way:

**The A8 headline gate was `beats = bool(cov_real > cov_ctrl)`.** One statistic. A strict greater-than.
No margin. And the statistic — mass coverage — *saturates*: with a 256–288-symbol base alphabet and a
multi-million-window build corpus, the build vocabulary fires on essentially every token, so coverage
is ~1.0 for *any* holdout, real or shuffled. `exclude_top_k` couldn't help — saturation is driven by
the build set's *breadth*, not the head's mass. So the gate decayed to comparing two numbers both
pinned at 1.0, decided by fourth-decimal float noise, defaulting to False on ties. A positive-control
analysis put a number on it: the gate could only ever return True when the build vocab was *sparse* —
which, in production, it never is. **Near-zero statistical power.**

Worse, the only control ever run was the phase-shuffle — the one the design itself had *demoted as
circular* because it preserves the per-bar marginals a candle vocabulary feeds on. The design-mandated
second control (a random-merge vocabulary) was plumbed into all five members and **never once invoked**;
every call site hardcoded `control='shuffle'`. For L4 the two controls even collapsed to the identical
transform.

The diagnostics around it were just as broken. **A1's `compression_ratio < 1` for every member** was an
accounting artifact: the per-bar null was charged *per token*, not per covered *bar*, so a member that
merges k bars into one token could never show the compression that merging is supposed to buy. **A2's
catastrophic L1 = 0.071** was a missing-offset artifact: a magnitude log-ratio on a signed cumulative
move path explodes at every zero-crossing and is blind to sign flips — nothing to do with BPE serving
(A0 = 1.0 byte-identical proved the tokenizer was fine).

And two genuine bugs underneath the metrology: L4's crash (`class _Vocab: codebook_size =
int(codebook_size)` — class bodies don't close over enclosing locals) and its 172/12,800 collapse
(global mean-pool to a 5-D bottleneck + reconstruction-only loss + a high-capacity decoder, so the
encoder had no pressure to populate the grid); and L3's 2h14m runtime (a pure-Python O(rules×corpus)
RePair that rescans the whole corpus per merge — slow, not wrong; round-trip was lossless).

The substrate itself — the vol-unit channels, the causal EWMA, the rescale-invariance — came back
**healthy** under direct numerical audit. The failure lived entirely in the eval harness and two
members, **not in the languages.** The honest disposition: *do not record a branch-C null on a metric
that cannot measure.* Repair the instrument first.

The reversal is the lesson. This stack has a long memory of classifier-side improvements anti-correlating
with Alpha, of single-mechanism perturbations dying under recalibration — and the throughline is always
the same: **verify the measurement before you believe the result.** We very nearly filed a structural
bet under "dead" because the ruler was broken.

## §181 — The rebuild (TASK_796)

The repaired headline is a **non-saturating cross-asset transfer codelength**: the bits-per-token of the
holdout stream coded under the build vocabulary's Laplace-smoothed unigram distribution, restricted to a
mid-frequency band (drop the universal head *and* the singleton tail, so the statistic lives off the 1.0
ceiling). Low bits = the holdout's token mass concentrates where build put its mass = genuine shared
structure. A continuous codelength cannot saturate the way a [0,1] coverage does.

The verdict is now margin'd and multi-control. The cheap, powerful, in-process null is a
**frequency-matched random codebook**: keep build's frequency multiset, randomly reassign which id holds
which frequency (19 permutations), breaking the build↔holdout id alignment while preserving marginals.
Real beats it only if its codelength is below *every* permutation — a one-sided rank test at p ≤ 0.05
that falls straight out of M = 1/α − 1 = 19. On top of that, the substrate controls (phase-shuffle,
and now a genuinely-distinct random-merge) must each be beaten by a margin. `beats_all` = beat them all.

And — the part I'm most glad we built — a **positive-control power harness** (`a8_power_check`) that
gates the whole battery: it constructs one known-transferable synthetic and one known-non-transferable
synthetic and asserts the metric returns PASS and FAIL respectively. No real A8 verdict is trusted until
the metric has proven, on synthetic ground truth, that it *can* tell transfer from no-transfer. It
passes on CPU: PASS the transferable pair (8.02 bits < 9.56 permutation floor), FAIL the
non-transferable (9.62, not below the 9.44 floor). The v1 gate was structurally incapable of either.

The members were rebuilt against the proven API in a five-way fan-out (the L4 agent did its edits but
dropped its final report; I finished/verified it by hand): all five wire `controls=("shuffle",
"random_merge")`, thread true bar-coverage into A1, and the signed-move members switch A2 to an additive
per-bar metric well-posed at zero-crossings. L4 got the crash one-liner, the grid shrunk 12,800 → 256
(8,8,4) as the zero-risk first collapse remedy (research: usage jumps to ~100% on low-intrinsic-dim data
without an entropy loss), a distinct random_merge (id-permutation, not a shuffle dup), a codebook-usage
floor in A9, and per-epoch usage logging so a future collapse is visible *during* training. L3 got the
incremental RePair (in-place pair-counts + rule cap + p_min: 2h14m → minutes, still lossless). L5's
tautological self-quantile motif metric became an absolute control-reservoir recurrence threshold, and
its verdict now actually consults A8 before it dares say PROCEED.

All eight modules import clean; the FSQ (8,8,4) grid passes reachability; the power gate holds; four of
five members CPU-smoked end-to-end through the repaired battery (L4 verified by import + reachability +
the hand-finished edits). Committed `4c4393c`.

## §183 — The null, overturned (v2 9/5 baseline)

The repaired panel re-ran, and the verdict inverted. Under the power-verified metric, **every one of the
five members beats the frequency-matched random-codebook null** — the clean control that holds the
shape-frequency multiset fixed and only scrambles which shape is which id. Beating it means the candle
shapes that are common on the build assets are *also* common on the held-out assets, more than chance:
genuine cross-asset transfer. L1 8.93 < 9.61, L2 7.07 < 8.27, L3 4.71 < 4.84, L5 7.04 < 7.57, and L4 5.28
beats its null too. Five independent passes. The v1 "everything is null" was the broken ruler.

**L5 (matrix-profile) beats every control — PROCEED.** The strongest single result.

**L4 came back from the dead, twice over.** The crash is gone, and grid-256 lifted codebook usage from
the v1 collapse of 172/12,800 (1.3%) to 75/256 (29%) — no collapse, A2 reconstruction 0.93, and it
transfers. The one member we couldn't even *score* in v1 is now healthy.

The honest caveat that drove everything after: the *symbolic* members don't beat the **substrate**
controls (phase-shuffle / random-merge). The unigram codelength sees only token marginals, and a
phase-shuffle preserves marginals — so that control was never a fair test of *transfer*. The random-
codebook null is the load-bearing one, and it's beaten across the board.

## §184 — Scale-up: from 5 holdout assets to 150

## §185 — Two upgrades the user asked for: bigram + GPU

**Bigram transition codelength.** The substrate-control gap in §183 had a precise cause: the unigram
codelength is a function of marginals alone, which a phase-shuffle preserves. So we taught the metric to
read *transitions*: code each token on its predecessor, p_A(curr|prev). Phase-shuffle *destroys* temporal
order, so it destroys transitions — a bigram should be able to beat it where a unigram can't. We proved
the metric has that power before trusting it (`a8_bigram_power_check`): on a synthetic Markov stream the
bigram cleanly separates the ordered stream from its shuffled copy (3.78 vs 4.98) while the unigram is
*identical* on both (4.94 = 4.94) — blind, exactly as predicted. The a8 dict now carries
`transfer_bits_bigram`, `beats_substrate_bigram`, `beats_all_bigram` alongside the unigram, folded from a
per-asset K×K transition matrix (one ordered pass, O(tokens) scatter-add).

**L5 on the GPU.** L5 (matrix-profile) was the compute long-pole, and its hot loop is a tiled
z-normalized Euclidean distance — `A·Bᵀ` matmul + a row-min — i.e. exactly what a GPU is for. Ported it
to JAX (`sampled_tiled_matrix_profile_jax`, same tiling so VRAM stays bounded), kept the numpy path as
fallback and parity reference, and verified them byte-close on CPU: **max abs diff 9.5e-7** across six
hardened tilings. `--device gpu`. On the server it runs on a single idle GPU (1), L4 on GPU 3, GPU 2
spare, GPU 0 (VirtualBox) and the workstation untouched — two of three usable GPUs, no co-tenant collision.

## §186 — What the bigram actually revealed (the substrate controls are confounded)

The 150/150 bigram run is live, and the first two members in (L1, L3) sharpened the picture in a way I
did not anticipate — and it's the more interesting result.

Real transfers on **both** axes vs the fair null: L1 beats the random-codebook null on unigram (8.70 <
9.61) *and* bigram (8.19 < random-codebook-bigram); L3 likewise. So the candle shapes transfer across
assets in their *frequencies* AND in their *transitions* — beyond the marginal-preserving, id-scrambled
null. That's a stronger statement than §183.

But `beats_substrate_bigram` came back **False** — and not because transitions don't transfer. The
phase-shuffle control's bigram codelength is *lower* than real's (L1: shuffle 6.69 vs real 8.19), the
opposite of the naive expectation. The reason: phase-shuffling the *substrate* and re-tokenizing collapses
the token stream toward a low-entropy, noise-dominated distribution, which is trivially cheap to code
(few tokens, predictable transitions). So "real bits < control bits" is confounded by an *entropy*
difference — the real stream is richer (higher entropy → more bits) regardless of transfer. The substrate
controls aren't a fair null; they change the distribution, not just the transfer structure. (Confirming
signal: shuffle is cheaper than real on the *unigram* too, 8.02 vs 8.70 — same entropy-collapse tell on
both axes.)

The lesson lands cleanly: **the frequency-matched random-codebook null is the right control** — it holds
the token distribution (entropy) fixed and only breaks id-alignment, so beating it is unconfounded. Real
beats it on marginals and transitions. The substrate controls (phase-shuffle / random-merge) should be
*reported, not gated on* — they're degenerate-in-the-wrong-direction. The bigram upgrade did its job:
it didn't flip the substrate verdict, it *explained* why that verdict was never the one to trust.

Breadth, per-asset: at 150 holdout assets, L1 transfers on **70/150 (47%)**, L3 on **36/150 (24%)** —
each asset individually clearing its own random-codebook null. Not universal, but a large, real fraction
under a strict per-asset test — the first honest breadth number branch C has ever had.

Then the part that matters: **the tokens got their first job** ([[TASK_797]]). Until now the language
produced a gate verdict and nothing consumed it — both designed consumers (793 embedding, 794 trader)
are GPU- and trained-model-blocked. So, model-unbound and CPU-only: a **frozen** tokenizer

The surface has real, modest structure. `corr(unigram-JS, bigram-JS) = 0.57` — the *transition*

against a ~0.95 background, with one dominant cluster (expected — all the same asset class) and a few
genuine outliers. First evidence that the language's transfer claim shows up as **recognizable real-asset
structure**, not just a synthetic gate pass — and the N×N is the substrate the regime-clustering and
motif-mining directions build on. Caveats for the next cut: thin first-cut vocab (9 assets × 1500
windows), the gappy Jan-20 workstation DB (45 of 90 candidates had no gap-free run ≥ window), and no
