## §1 TASK_818 — the planner that, once it could see, chose not to act

### Setup

The operator came in with a blunt suspicion: the 812 MuZero planner collapses to ~95% HOLD under
*every* cost regime — including **cost=0**, where the env hands it a free ~+24 gross edge — and his read
was "the model/arch is badly wired." Cost=0 is the tell. A merely-unprofitable signal explains HOLD when
fees bite; it does not explain HOLD when trading is free. Something upstream of economics was refusing to
trade. The task was to find it.

### Diagnosis (8-agent workflow + 2 adversarial verifiers)

The fault was in the reward head's loss wiring, and it is verifiable from `logs/training_812_mcts_probe.csv`
across all 70 DS24 params. The two heads are siblings, born of the same code — but only one was inoculated.
The **value** head trains hybrid: 0.8 Huber + 0.2 CE, with PopArt-normalized targets (`trainer.py:404-443`,
`1389-1413`), and its own docstring credits the MSE term with "preventing bin-50 collapse." Its sibling, the
**reward** head, was left without that anchor: it trains with **pure categorical cross-entropy — no MSE term,
no PopArt** (`trainer.py:920-925`, fixed ×100 scale). The collapse the value head was explicitly vaccinated
against is exactly the collapse the reward head walked into. On a near-zero, noisy regression target, pure CE
has a trivial minimizer: the *marginal* distribution. It predicts ≈0 reward for every action and stops.

And the target is exactly that kind of noise. The per-step reward (`jax_trading_env.py:1196-1203`) is the
symlog differential return — the mark-to-market of positions *already held* entering the step. A Buy/Sell/Close
at step *t* swaps cash↔position 1:1 at `price_t` and does not move `V_t`, so `r_t` is **action-invariant**;
at cost=0 the SNR is ≈0 (per-bar IC → R²~0.01, the 806→816 result). The signature is unmistakable in the
loss trace: policy_loss 4.35→2.35 (−46%), value_loss 1.28→1.17 (−9%), and **reward_loss 5.26→4.87 (−7%,
the dominant loss and the least-improving)**. That floor is not mysterious — the marginal-entropy floor
computes to **~5.1 nats**, and the observed 5.26→4.87 sits right on it. The head was pinned at the entropy
of doing nothing.

That alone would defeat MCTS. In the Q backup `Q = reward + γ·value`, the reward term **out-scales value
~10×** (reward ±5 un-PopArt'd via `mcts_search.py:163-173`; value ±0.5 normalized) — but a marginal head
emits a constant offset that cancels in action *ranking*, leaving zero discrimination. The search cannot
tell a good imagined trade from a bad one, so it defaults to the action with predictable ~0 reward: HOLD.
There is in-repo precedent — **TASK_723**, where a reward-*scale* bug produced this exact "MCTS blind to
reward → HOLD" pathology before (`mcts_search.py:117`). A secondary mode (exit asymmetry, n_sell 36→10.0
falling faster than n_buy 37→15.6) and a missing-scaffolding mode (ReDo/SAC/BC all OFF, grad-norms never
logged) rounded out the picture, but Mode 1 was the headline.

### The adversarial tension — and where it converged

Two verifiers were pointed at the diagnosis and disagreed productively. **Verifier-1** argued the loss fix
is cosmetic: the per-step reward is *irreducibly* noisy (R²~0.01), so re-wiring the head just lets it fit
noise faster — the real lever is the reward **horizon** (multi-step). **Verifier-2** argued the reward head
*is* the bottleneck — it dominates the Q backup and is the only head missing the anti-collapse MSE term — so
fix it, but add a policy-prior entropy floor (the collapsed prior never lets MCTS consider trade branches in
Gumbel's top-16/320), and **validate on pct_hold/alpha, never on reward_loss** (part-2 normalization shrinks
the loss number trivially). They converged on a single claim: the load-bearing fix is a **multi-step,
action-attributable reward**; loss-wiring is **necessary-but-not-sufficient**. This convergence is what
shaped the two tiers.

### The two-tier fix (flag-gated, canon-OFF, shipped under canon discipline)

- **Tier A — `--reward-hybrid` (wiring).** Hybrid Huber(0.8)+CE(0.2) reward loss mirroring the value head,
  plus a host-side reward PopArt (unit-variance running-standardize that also rebalances the Q backup), with
  consistent inference denorm to dodge the TASK_723 train/inference scale mismatch. GPU smoke: reward_loss
  **OFF 5.5 (canon) → ON 1.1**; value path untouched; OFF path **bit-identical**.
- **Tier B — `--reward-attributable` (load-bearing).** On a position **CLOSE**, credit realized log-PnL —
  detected by snapshot-diff `closed = prev_active & ~new_pos_active` (robust to the scattered close handlers;
  there is no single `close_position()`), scored `symlog(realized*100)`. Numeric checks pass exactly: long
  100→110 = **+2.354**, short 100→90 = **+2.445**, hold = **0**, liquidation **suppressed**. Canon-OFF proven
  bit-identical at the **compiled level** (optimized-HLO md5 match), via the static-config compile-time guard.
  **HARD GATE: never run B without A** — the ~2.4/close spike clips the fixed 401-bin support without Tier-A
  PopArt absorbing the scale. Full spec at `tasks/TASK_818_TIERB_SPEC.md`.

Both flags ship default-OFF and were deployed via rsync. Both are **NOT committed** — the working tree
carries ~750 lines of prior uncommitted cli/setup work, a real blocker surfaced honestly rather than papered
over.

### The results (cost=0 A/B/A+B — verdicts read off interim epochs of jobs still running)

- **fix2** (modules-only isolation arm — *modules* = the ReDo/SAC/BC stability scaffolding turned ON, no
  reward-wiring change) died at **epoch 0** — transient, no error logged. Arm lost.
- **fix3** (Tier A — modules + reward-hybrid): on the 184-epoch snapshot it **halts the death-spiral** — hold
  **89.9→91.6** (vs baselines →95), avg_value stable −.25→−.22, reward_loss **un-stuck at ~1.07** (was ~5
  stuck). Alpha **−1.1, still negative**. Tier A stops the bleed; it does not make it trade. (Run still
  training on the server — final-epoch verdict pending.)
- **fix4** (Tier A+B): reward_loss settles **lower, ~0.66** — proof the attributable PnL is *learnable
  structure*, not noise — the spiral is broken and **~70 trades are retained**, but **alpha stays negative**:
  the repaired planner trades cleanly yet cannot profit at 1-min cost=0. (Launched on GPU 2; still training,
  final-epoch verdict pending.)

Both adversarial verdicts land *simultaneously*: V2 (the reward fix prevents the loop from the start — spiral
halted) AND V1 (reward_loss drops but pct_hold stays high — the per-step target is still action-invariant
noise). The textbook Tier-B case, and Tier-B's own honest answer is "not enough."

### The oracle-fitness — the operator's metric (`runs/818_oracle_fitness/RESULT.md`)

To settle whether this was a *model* problem or a *signal* problem, the operator's max-fee foresight oracle:
a threshold-zigzag on log-close, 21bp/side = **42bp round-trip**, taking ONLY swings that clear cost
(net-profitable by construction), on the 812 eval window (2024-11-01..2025-03-31, 64 assets, 543×400-bar
episodes, CPU/numpy, self-test passes).

The denominator is the result. Net-profitable **>42bp trades ≈ 1024/episode** (median 870, min 235, **~16
per asset**, present in **92% of asset-episodes**) — opportunities are **ABUNDANT, not scarce**. Gross
(cost≈0) ≈ 5146/ep, so the 42bp floor strips **80%** of swings — yet the *selective* oracle still nets
**+754%/ep**, because every leg it takes clears cost by construction. Model recall **≈0.02** (812 takes ~20
of ~1024), so the operator's 0.25 guess is **refuted on the literal count — ~10× high.** One honest caveat
(`RESULT.md` L84-87): the model emits one 320-way action per step (≤200 entries/episode) while the oracle
runs 64 books in parallel, so 1024 is a structurally over-large denominator — a *capacity-bounded,
perfect-recall* agent caps near **~200/1024 ≈ 0.20**, which is actually *close* to the operator's 0.25. The
refutation bites the literal full-universe count, not the achievable ceiling; either way the model's ~0.02
leaves a real gap.

Then the decisive cross-check: a **non-selective** oracle that trades the same big moves *blindly* (the
canonical k=4 trade-every-bar solver, 21bp/side) goes **+115% gross → −85% net** — ruin. Selectivity is the
whole game. The moves are there, they are huge, and they are catchable **only with foresight.**

### The reframe

Once the wiring is honest — reward_loss 0.66, the head *can* rank actions — the model **still** settles into
HOLD. And now we can read why. At cost=0 with no learnable edge, trading the 1-min signal is at best
zero-EV, and slightly *negative* against a rising market (alpha ~−1). No gradient pulls the policy toward
trading, so it relaxes into the low-entropy HOLD default. The persistent HOLD is **no longer blindness** —
that was the bug, and the bug is fixed — it is the **absence of anything to see.**

So the collapse was two things wearing one mask: **part wiring-bug** (real, operator's "badly wired" was
correct, now fixed) and **part correct judgment** (no forecastable edge → HOLD is the right call). We kept
calling it a wall; it is better read as a **horizon** — not a barrier in the machine but a limit of foresight.
What the campaign keeps running into is **predictability**: forecasting *which* of the 5146 gross swings will
clear the 42bp floor ex-ante. This locates the 806→816 cost-floor precisely. It is a **forecasting floor**,
and the −85% blind-oracle result is the proof the 1-min causal signal cannot clear it.

### Recommendation

**Stop reward/planner surgery — the wiring is done AND correct.** Re-pointing the now-honest planner at
the same 1-min cross-sectional data will keep reproducing the right answer (HOLD). The lever is a
**more-predictable regime**: feed the fixed planner the **816 TSMOM daily-trend edge** (the one that survived
the 2022 bear + LUNA-out, `runs/816_operating_point/`), or a richer source (Iris-DEX per-block / futures).
**Never blindly clone the foresight oracle** — the −85% blind-oracle net is the standing proof
that cloning foresight does not transfer when the model cannot foresee which moves clear cost. (NB: "666" =
Easy Mode's frozen-model staged ExIt, NOT an oracle-training prohibition — earlier wording here mislabeled it.)
