**The channel deep-dive (`mcts_trader/env/jax_encoder.py:491-535`)** — the decision-relevant find:

The 5 cross-sectional channels (27–31: rank of 1-step return, rank of 20-step cumulative return, beta to the equal-weighted universe, idiosyncratic vol, cross-sectional momentum) are computed as **input preprocessing** — `argsort`/`mean` over the 64-asset axis, written into the `(32, ws, n)` input tensor before the model sees anything. `n_f = n−1` (`:498`) and the fixed 32-wide channel axis hard-code n=64. So the 64-asset universe is baked into the input *tensor*, not just the model — which is exactly what blocks variable-universe, per-asset, and dataset-transfer approaches. It is also why 738_legacy_27ch had to *delete* the signal to test it (−18.44pp): there was no way to keep the signal while removing the coupling.

**The de-risk:** these 5 features are deterministic functions of the input prices — no parameters, no gradient target. They can be **relocated into the encoder forward pass**, computed from the per-asset channels at the same temporal resolution. If the relocation reproduces the same values, the encoder is bit-identical on the 64-asset universe — validatable by spot-check, no Alpha claim attached. The channel restructure is therefore an **Alpha-neutral unblocker**: it turns the asset universe from a frozen assumption into a runtime dimension. That decouples it from any Alpha experiment and makes it cheap to ship and verify. The Alpha question then moves downstream to whatever the unblocking enables — variable universe, 765 dataset transfer, per-asset inference.

## §163 — F2: the PopArt pre-clip, confirmed by rs200

F2 was the third finding of the Gemini bughunt (`TASK_770`), downgraded P1→P2 and deferred post-762. 762 Wave 1 promoted it from "latent design smell" to **confirmed, with evidence**. Recording it in full — it now explains the shape of a real result and changes how Wave 1 is read.

**What F2 is.** The Phase-2 reward/value pipeline applies a hard `[−10, 10]` clip to its targets *before* PopArt normalization. For the canon `nstep` value-target mode (`train_phase2.py`):

```
per_step_pnl × reward_scale          # :679
  → clip[−10, 10]                    # :680   ← CLIP, pre-PopArt
  → discounted N-step sum
  → + γ^N · V(s_{t+N})  = td_target_raw
  → clip[−10, 10]                    # :1213  ← CLIP, pre-PopArt
  → (· − popart_mean) / popart_std   # :1214  PopArt normalize
```

(The `oracle` mode has the same shape — `oracle_scaled` clipped at `:1198`, normalized at `:1199`. The immediate-reward path clips at `:637`.)

**Why "pre-clip" is the bug.** PopArt (van Hasselt 2016) exists precisely so the value head can learn from targets spanning orders of magnitude — `popart_mean`/`popart_std` track running statistics and adaptively rescale; the head learns in normalized space. The whole point is that you do *not* hand-scale or hand-clip the target. Clipping `[−10, 10]` *upstream* of PopArt censors the dynamic range PopArt was built to absorb — it defeats the mechanism it sits in front of.

The `[−10, 10]` itself is not arbitrary: it is the categorical value head's support (401 bins over `[−10, +10]`). A target outside that range cannot be encoded as a categorical distribution. But that representability constraint applies to the *normalized* target — the thing the head actually predicts — not to the raw, pre-PopArt target. The clip enforces a normalized-space constraint in raw space, one step too early.

**The reward_scale coupling.** `reward_scale` multiplies the PnL and *then* the clip bites: `clip(step_pnl × reward_scale, −10, 10)`. The two are not independent knobs. At `reward_scale=100` (canon) a 10% step → `0.10 × 100 = 10`, exactly the clip edge. At `reward_scale=200` a 5% step already saturates, and anything larger is censored to a flat ±10. Sweeping `reward_scale` upward necessarily pushes more of the reward distribution into the clip — you cannot test "bigger reward signal" without also testing "more aggressive censorship."

**The evidence — rs200.** 762 Wave 1's sweep: rs025 +0.22pp, rs050 +0.27pp, **rs200 −1.33pp (all 3 seeds negative)**. The monotonic collapse at the high end is the clip signature — low scale keeps the reward distribution inside `[−10, 10]` so the clip rarely bites (near-neutral Δα); high scale saturates it (regression). The F2 bughunt predicted exactly this *in the abstract*, before any 762 cell ran. Wave 1 confirmed it empirically.

**Consequence for reading Wave 1.** The `reward_scale` sweep is **confounded by F2 at the high end**. "reward_scale is a real but blunt lever" is sound *for the clip-clean low end* — rs025/rs050 genuinely move trade-rate the right way and Alpha barely. But rs200's regression should be attributed to the **clip**, not to reward_scale being intrinsically toxic at 200. Wave 2 (`fee_penalty_bps` sweep at rs050) runs at low scale → F2-clean, no reinterpretation needed there.

**The fix — experiment 769, a proper chain link.** F2 is not a deferred bugfix — it is a value-pipeline structural *experiment*: `experiments/769_value_clip/SPEC.md`. Implemented **flag-gated** (`value_clip_mode`, default `pre_popart` = canon, bit-identical) — the same discipline as 762/763/767/F1 — so the code deploys safely mid-campaign and only the *experiment run* is sequenced. The fix: PopArt-normalize first, then clip the *normalized* target to the support range — PopArt absorbs the scale, the clip only catches genuine post-normalization outliers. Two variants, 3 seeds: `769-fix-rs100` (Alpha lift at canon scale vs 760 a1b1) and `769-fix-rs200` (does the fix rescue the cell the clip broke, vs Wave 1 rs200). It is the **conclusion of the 762 reward-shaping arc** — it answers what Wave 1 could not: was rs200's collapse the `reward_scale` lever failing, or the clip censoring it. Recommended server slot: immediately after 762 Wave 2. Pre-existing design — `b4e0158` (TASK_762) did not introduce F2; it added the `reward_scale` knob that made the latent clip *reachable*, and the bughunt + Wave 1 then exposed it.
