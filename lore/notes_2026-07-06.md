## §1 The crash night — three hard resets, one root cause

The box went down three times in 35 minutes, and the June diagnosis did not survive the night.

Timeline (journal boot table, CEST): **22:54:38** sync flood #1 — mixed 4-DIMM RAM, 821 attempt-1
training at **~ep59** (`runs/821_planner_daily/train_cost0.log` ends mid-episode-59). Operator
dropped DRAM to 2400 in BIOS (boot 22:55–23:06, then 23:14–23:20 — the 2400 setting would not
survive the later stick swap). **23:25** operator pulled the two 8GB HyperX sticks per the June
plan — matched 2×16GB FURY pair only — and **23:30:08** sync flood #2 hit ~5 min into attempt-4's
data load, **on the matched pair**. The June "mixed-kit root cause" hypothesis
([[workstation-syncflood-crashes]]) was falsified on the spot: at most an aggravator.

Decisive forensics (`tasks/TASK_822_WORKSTATION_STABILITY.md`): the MCE records are
**bit-identical across both RAM configs** — `Bank 0` (Zen3 Load-Store), status
`baa00000000c0135`, SYND `4d000014`, IPID `1000b000000000` (crash #1 mixed / CPU 9; crash #4
matched / CPU 1). Same fault fingerprint, different DIMMs → the fault sits in the CPU/SoC domain,
not in a stick. For twenty minutes the leading hypothesis was a degrading 5800X.

Then the twist. Operator ran `dmidecode`: the sticks were in **DIMM_A1 + DIMM_B1 — the wrong
slots** for 2-DIMM on the PRIME B550-PLUS daisy-chain topology (validated config is A2+B2;
A1+B1 is the electrically worst arrangement) — and BIOS had **silently reverted DRAM to 3200**
after the swap. Both crash configs of the night were bad topologies. The identical LS-bank MCE is
consistent with bad data arriving over a marginal memory path; the fingerprint named the organ
that *received* the corruption, not the one that caused it.

Fix applied **~00:15** at one power-off (BIOS research in `runs/822_stability/BIOS_CHEATSHEET.md`):
sticks → **A2+B2**; BIOS **3634 → 3641 Beta (AGESA ComboV2 PI 1.2.0.12**, 2026-06-30); **Power
Supply Idle Control → Typical Current Idle** (the canonical Ryzen idle-crash fix — June's floods
fired at idle too); VSOC/VDDG set per cheatsheet. Validation on the new config passed clean:
`tools/ram_stress_verify.py` checksum loop **19 passes @16GB, 0 corruption**
(`runs/822_stability/ram_stress_newconfig.log`) + **stress-ng 25 min, 0 failures, full boost,
Tctl peak ~84°C** (`stress_cpu_newconfig.log`). Notable control: the *pre-fix* ladder on A1/B1@3200
also ran 18 clean passes without reproducing a flood (`ram_stress.log`) — the fault does not yield
to a simple load test, which is exactly why the June diagnosis went wrong.

One more find with a hole in it: **rasdaemon has never recorded anything since its June install**
(empty DB — every "next fault will decode" promise in the June notes was void). The unit now
carries `-f -r` (record mode), but the daemon itself **ABRT-loops on a startup buffer overflow**
— on the current boot it burned its 5 systemd restarts by 00:17:45 and is `failed`;
`/var/lib/rasdaemon/` is still empty as of 08:30. **NOT fixed, still an open 822 item** — the next
MCE will again arrive undecoded unless this is repaired or replaced (mcelog / manual
`journalctl -k` triage).

Lesson: an identical MCE fingerprint across component swaps localizes the *victim*, not the
*culprit* — check topology and silent BIOS reverts before condemning silicon.

## §4 TASK_821 pass-1 — the clean cell fills, and closes the planner

Attempt 5 (seed 42, cost=0, post-fix hardware) is the **first-ever completion** of the clean-cell
run: **2000 epochs in 6h13m**, checkpoint `jax_ckpt_fast/821_planner_daily/checkpoint_100000`,
log `runs/821_planner_daily/train_cost0_r5.log` (attempts 1–4 all killed by §1's crashes;
per-attempt logs preserved).

Training-side, the run was *healthy* — the thing every prior planner run was not. **No death
spiral**: policy entropy fell to ~1.06 mid-run and **rose to 1.88** through the back half;
reward_loss lived at ~0.75–0.87 (all-run mean 0.79 — never the stuck-at-5 floor of the pre-818
wiring); non-hold actions per episode recovered from a mid-run trough (~3) into the teens–30s.
The episode alpha column even reproduced the bull-blindness silhouette — positive α in bear
episodes, negative in bulls — with **no oracle teacher and no differential reward anywhere in the
loop**, which briefly looked like the old disease surviving decontamination.

Then the eval (`runs/821_planner_daily/EVAL_PASS1.md`; deterministic full-timeline rollout
2021→2025, 1670 daily decisions, BOTH det-MCTS sims=32 and masked policy-argmax):

- **100% HOLD. 0 / 1670 trades. Both decode modes identical** → the prior itself peaks on HOLD;
  not a search artifact (all 320 actions unmasked).
- **net@21 Sharpe +0.00 vs the pre-registered +0.97 linear bar. B&H +125.95% unbeaten. BEAR22
  +0.00, not positive.** Every pre-registered fail condition fired at once.
- The training trades were **exploration noise only** — strip gumbel and the ~3% non-hold
  vanishes. And the bull-blindness silhouette dissolves with it: at near-zero net exposure,
  α ≈ −market_return. The silhouette was the market's sign flipped, not behavior.
- Fee pass (pass 2) **deliberately not run** — costs can only strengthen a conclusion reached at
  cost zero. In-sample, which also strengthens it: it cannot act even on the data it trained on.

The pre-registered **"CLOSES the planner"** branch fired — with all five historical
contaminations removed (818 wiring fixed, daily bars, absolute PnL, no oracle teacher,
multi-regime incl. BEAR22). "MCTS cannot trade this data" is now a measured statement with no
asterisk, six weeks after it was first suspected on contaminated evidence.

The architectural reading (memory: [[project_planner_closed_clean_cell]], status updated in
`tasks/TASK_821_PLANNER_DAILY_CLEAN_CELL.md`): the daily crypto edge (IC≈0.03) exists only in
**aggregate** — the 819 linear book harvests it through breadth, dozens of tiny simultaneous
positions whose individual EV is under water relative to noise. A sequential value-based planner
asks, per decision, "is THIS trade's expected value above my uncertainty?" — and the honest
answer, every single time, is no. The planner didn't fail to find the edge; it correctly priced
each trade alone and declined. The edge lives at the portfolio level, below the per-decision
detection threshold. That is a statement about problem structure, not about MuZero.

Lesson: a breadth-aggregated, below-noise-per-decision edge is structurally invisible to
sequential per-decision EV maximizers — match the architecture to where the edge lives, or
change the data until per-decision EV clears the bar.
