### Deep Analysis: Three Independent Audits

Launched Explore + 2× Opus agents (~300k tokens total) for comprehensive code audit.

**Critical findings — MCTS is functionally broken:**

1. **Reward /100.0 descaling** (`mctx_search.py:110`): Reward head outputs [-10, 10], divided by 100 → ~0.001. Value ≈ 2.5. Reward is 0.04% of Q-value. **MCTS is completely blind to rewards.** Additionally, `_categorical_to_scalar` applies PopArt scale/shift to rewards (which aren't PopArt-normalized) — corrupts reward signal further.

2. **Discount mismatch**: MCTS uses γ=0.997 but value trained with γ=0.95. Systematic overvaluation of future states in tree search.

3. **max_considered_actions=16 out of 320**: Top-16 prior actions are all HOLD variants. BUY/SELL never explored. Creates self-reinforcing HOLD feedback loop.

4. **BC weight too low (0.005)**: Oracle signal (which is correct) has 200× less influence than MCTS (which is uninformative). Policy was BETTER at E93 (1.71, BC-driven) than E999 (1.93, MCTS-driven). **MCTS is actively hurting the policy.**

5. **No slot rotation**: Same 17 segments for 1000 epochs. 140 available, 17 used.

### TASK_724: Factored Per-Asset Search — SPEC WRITTEN

Joint MCTS is structurally broken for 64×5 factored action space — even with TASK_723's max_considered=64, MCTS treats independent per-asset decisions as competing joint alternatives. C(64,4) ≈ 635K possible 4-asset combinations, MCTS explores 64.

**Factored search design**: Replace mctx tree search with 1-step per-asset Q-evaluation:
- For each of 320 (asset, action) pairs: Q(i,a) = R(i,a) + γ * V(next_state)
- Batched as one forward pass of B×320 `recurrent_inference` calls (sub-batched for VRAM)
- Per-asset softmax of Q-values → policy targets (each group of 5 sums to 1.0)
- Per-asset softmax CE loss (not global CE) — matches BC loss structure

**Key details**:
- Same `__call__` interface as `MuZeroMCTXSearch` — drop-in replacement
- Rewards: raw categorical→scalar, NO PopArt, NO /100 (handles correctly unlike mctx path)
- Values: PopArt denormalized (value_scale/value_shift set per-epoch)
- Sub-batch size 4096: ~200MB peak VRAM, ~80s/epoch (full reasoning) or ~4s/epoch (value-head only)
- Discount MUST be td_discount=0.95 (not mcts_discount)

**Checkpoint compatible**: No architecture changes. Resume from E982 with `reset_value_head=false`.

### Plan: Ship 723 + 724 Together

Decision: skip intermediate 723-only run. Deep analysis conclusively showed joint MCTS is broken even with all fixes. 723 fixes are prerequisites (reward handling, discount, BC, rotation) but insufficient alone. Implementing both saves a 14-hour training run.

Execution order:

## Session 53 — Encoder Direction Audit (2026-04-17/18)

### Root Cause Found: Oracle Class Imbalance, NOT Encoder

Launched encoder direction audit (`prompt-lib/encoder_direction_audit.md`) to trace where directional information gets destroyed. Results in `prompt-lib/out_encoder_audit/`.

**Key findings:**

1. **Direction IS preserved in observations** — 10+ of 27 market channels carry signed direction. Channel 15 (64-step momentum) is literally ±1 binary. Channels 7, 12-14 carry signed returns. Channels 20-23 flip 0↔1 between bull/bear.

2. **Trained encoder preserves direction** — bull vs bear latent cosine similarity = 0.384 (far from identical). ||diff|| = 59.7 with norms ~50-58. The 128-dim latent clearly distinguishes regime.

3. **Policy head outputs near-uniform regardless** — All 5 actions at ~20% (±1%) for both 20% rally and 20% crash. The directional signal in the latent doesn't reach the policy output.

4. **Root cause: 93.75% HOLD by construction** — Oracle selects k=4 of 64 assets → 93.75% HOLD floor. BUY vs SELL swing between bull and bear regimes is only ~1.2 percentage points. The BC loss fits the marginal (94% HOLD) and the conditional per-asset BUY/SELL signal drowns.

**Previous hypothesis was wrong**: Bull blindness is NOT caused by the encoder killing direction, the oracle being bad in bull, or MCTS exploration. It's caused by extreme class imbalance in the training target: the model learns "HOLD everything" because that's 94% of the oracle's output, and the remaining 6% BUY/SELL signal is too weak to learn through standard cross-entropy.

`trade_loss_weight=60` and `trade_sample_prob=0.3` exist but aren't sufficient to overcome 94% HOLD dominance.

### Proposed Fixes (from audit, ranked)

1. **Rebalance BC loss** — mask HOLD or use per-class weights (w_hold=1/16, w_buy=w_sell=1.0). Makes BUY/SELL gradient equal to HOLD gradient per-step. Zero architecture change.

2. **Auxiliary direction head** — small MLP predicting sign of next K-step return per asset. Dense directional supervision (64 binary labels per step vs oracle's 4-asset sparse selection). One new head, keeps encoder frozen.

3. **Unfreeze encoder** — let encoder fine-tune features toward direction. Low LR (1e-5). Last resort.

```

  OHLCV prices → encoder (27ch, 10+ directional) → latent (cosine 0.38 bull/bear)
       ✓ direction preserved              ✓ direction preserved

  → TRM reasoning (MLP H=6×L=8) → policy head (320 logits) → softmax → action
       ? direction used?              ✗ near-uniform output     ✗ defaults to HOLD

```

The bottleneck is between the latent (which has direction) and the policy output (which doesn't use it). The training loss doesn't provide enough gradient on the 6% minority class (BUY/SELL) for the policy to learn the conditional.
