Coupled Policy-Prediction Collapse in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning: A Comprehensive Analysis of Dual Failure Modes in Stochastic Trading Environments
1. Introduction: The Anatomy of Dual Collapse

The deployment of deep model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) agents in high-stakes, stochastic environments—specifically algorithmic trading—has revealed a class of systemic failure modes distinct from those observed in deterministic domains like board games or robotic control. The most pernicious of these is the "Coupled Policy-Prediction Collapse," a phenomenon where the agent's decision-making policy and its internal world model degrade simultaneously, entering a self-reinforcing feedback loop that halts learning.

This report addresses a specific instance of this pathology: a MuZero-style agent utilizing a Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (Neural ODE) prediction head for price forecasting. The observed symptomatology—a policy converging to a deterministic HOLD action and a prediction head converging to a trivial identity function—represents a fundamental breakdown in the exploration-exploitation mechanism.

The collapse is not merely a failure of hyperparameter tuning but a structural intersection of three deep learning pathologies: Entropy Collapse in actor-critic optimization , the Dormant Neuron Phenomenon in non-stationary learning , and the Trivial Solution Trap in differential equation modeling. This analysis synthesizes recent literature to deconstruct the causal loops driving this dual collapse and proposes a mathematically grounded remediation strategy.  

1.1 The Phenomenological Observation

In the specific case under investigation, the agent exhibits two synchronized degradations:

    Policy Entropy Decay: The policy distribution π(a∣s), initialized with high entropy (≈4.5), rapidly narrows to a Dirac delta distribution centered on the HOLD action. This occurs despite standard entropy regularization, indicating that the "safety" of inaction in a high-noise environment creates a basin of attraction deeper than the regularization can counteract.   

Prediction Mode Collapse: The auxiliary world model, tasked with predicting future states via a Neural ODE, exhibits a precipitous drop in Mean Squared Error (MSE) from 0.4 to 0.01. This reduction is illusory; the model has not learned market dynamics but has instead converged to the identity function s^t+1​≈st​, predicting "no change" for all inputs.  

1.2 The Feedback Loop Mechanism

The central thesis of this report is that these two collapses are causally linked through the data generation process.

    Forward Coupling (Model → Policy): In MuZero, policy improvement relies on Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) utilizing the learned dynamics model. When the model collapses to a trivial identity function, all simulated trajectories appear flat. The value function V(s) propagates a signal that state transitions yield no value differential. In the absence of predicted volatility, the optimal risk-averse policy is HOLD to avoid transaction costs.   

Backward Coupling (Policy → Model): Conversely, as the policy converges to HOLD, the replay buffer used to train the world model becomes homogenized. The model observes only trajectories where at​=HOLD. Deprived of causal interventions (what happens when we BUY?), the model overfits to the "no-action, no-change" regime, reinforcing the trivial solution.  

2. Policy Collapse: Mechanisms and Entropy Dynamics

Policy collapse, defined as the premature convergence of a policy to a deterministic sub-optimal behavior, is a well-documented failure mode in reinforcement learning. In the context of financial trading, this phenomenon is exacerbated by the specific reward structure and noise characteristics of the domain.  

2.1 Theoretical Formulation of Entropy Dynamics

In Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning, the objective is to maximize the expected return augmented by an entropy term:
J(π)=Eτ∼π​[t=0∑∞​γt(r(st​,at​)+αH(π(⋅∣st​)))]

where H(π(⋅∣st​)) is the Shannon entropy of the policy at state st​, and α is the temperature parameter. The entropy term is intended to encourage exploration by penalizing deterministic policies. However, in practice, the gradient of the entropy term can be overwhelmed by the gradient of the advantage function.  

The "Hold" Attractor

In a trading MDP, the action space is typically A={Long,Short,Hold}. The reward structure usually includes explicit transaction costs c for entering or exiting positions.

    R(s,Buy)=ΔP−c

    R(s,Sell)=−ΔP−c

    R(s,Hold)=0 (assuming no position)

At initialization, the agent's predictions of ΔP are random (noise). The expected value of buying or selling is E−c≈−c. The expected value of holding is 0. Consequently, the Advantage Function A(s,a)=Q(s,a)−V(s) heavily favors holding:
A(s,Hold)>A(s,Buy)andA(s,Hold)>A(s,Sell)

The policy gradient update increases the probability of the action with the highest advantage. As π(Hold∣s)→1, the entropy H(π) approaches 0. Once the policy mass is concentrated on HOLD, the agent rarely samples BUY or SELL actions, preventing it from ever observing the potentially high positive rewards that would offset the transaction costs.  

2.2 The Dormant Neuron Phenomenon

A critical factor driving the irreversibility of this collapse is the Dormant Neuron Phenomenon, recently identified as a pervasive issue in deep RL.  

    Mechanism: During the early phases of training, as the agent rapidly learns to minimize negative rewards (transaction costs) by outputting HOLD, a significant portion of the neurons in the policy network's hidden layers become inactive. A neuron is considered "dormant" if its activation is consistently zero (dead ReLU) or negligible across the state distribution.   

Plasticity Loss: Research indicates that once neurons become dormant, they lose their ability to adapt to new gradients, even if the reward landscape changes (e.g., via entropy boosting). This results in a loss of network plasticity. The network effectively loses capacity, collapsing its representational power to a simple linear discriminator that outputs HOLD regardless of the input state.  

Impact on Reanalyze: MuZero employs "Reanalyze," where past trajectories are re-evaluated using the current network parameters to generate fresh policy targets. If the current network suffers from dormant neurons and outputs a collapsed policy, Reanalyze essentially overwrites the diverse historical data in the replay buffer with collapsed targets. This "data poisoning" accelerates the collapse, as the agent is retrained on its own degenerate output.  

2.3 Action Collapse in Latent Representations

Recent studies in Visual Reinforcement Learning (VRL) have identified a specific pathology termed Action Collapse, which is highly relevant to MuZero's latent space planning.  

    Layer-Specific Failure: Analysis using linear probing reveals that while the early convolutional layers of an agent may retain diverse feature representations, the later MLP layers (closer to the policy head) collapse to a single point representation for all states.

    Implication: The policy network becomes "blind" to state differences. It perceives a high-volatility market state and a low-volatility market state as identical representations in the penultimate layer. Consequently, it outputs the same action (HOLD) for both. This structural collapse renders standard exploration techniques (like epsilon-greedy) ineffective because the network cannot distinguish the states required to condition a complex policy.   

2.4 The Failure of Fixed Entropy Regularization

The user notes that "Adaptive entropy boosting temporarily reverses but doesn't prevent recurrence." This suggests a failure of the standard regularization formulation.

    Mathematical Conflict: In standard PPO/Actor-Critic, the entropy bonus is added to the loss: Lent​=βS[π]. If the advantage of HOLD (avoiding loss) is significantly larger than β, the optimizer will sacrifice entropy to secure the "safe" reward.

    Decay Schedules: Typical implementations decay the entropy coefficient over time. In a non-stationary trading environment, this is catastrophic. As the market regime shifts (e.g., from calm to volatile), the agent needs high entropy to re-explore, but the decayed coefficient forces it to stick to the outdated HOLD strategy.   

3. Prediction Collapse: Neural ODEs and the Trivial Solution

The auxiliary prediction head, implemented as a Neural ODE, is designed to model the continuous-time evolution of the market state. Its collapse to a trivial solution ("predict no change") is a distinct mathematical pathology rooted in the optimization of differential equations.
3.1 The Identity Trap in Differential Equations

A Neural ODE models the state transition as:
dtdz(t)​=fθ​(z(t),t)
z(t1​)=z(t0​)+∫t0​t1​​fθ​(z(t),t)dt

The training objective typically minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted state z(t1​) and the ground truth.

The Trivial Solution: In many physical and financial systems, the function f(z,t)=0 is a strong local minimum for the optimizer.  

    If fθ​(z,t)=0, then dtdz​=0, and z(t1​)=z(t0​).

    This corresponds to the identity mapping: predicting that the future price equals the current price.

    In financial time series, the "no change" prediction is often statistically superior to a random direction prediction (due to the Martingale property of efficient markets) and yields a low MSE, dominated only by the aleatoric noise (volatility).

Why Gradient Descent Prefers Identity: Learning the true, complex vector field of market dynamics requires the network to capture high-frequency oscillations. However, high-frequency components introduce stiffness to the ODE, making numerical integration unstable. The optimizer, penalizing instability and large errors, drives the weights of fθ​ toward zero to smooth the trajectory. The network learns that the safest path to low loss is to predict nothing happens.  

3.2 Flow Matching vs. Integration

Standard Neural ODE training involves backpropagating through the ODE solver (using the adjoint method). This process is computationally expensive and prone to gradient instability (vanishing/exploding gradients).

    Crossing Trajectories: A fundamental limitation of ODEs is that trajectories cannot cross in the phase space (uniqueness of solutions). However, in the projected latent space of a trading agent, different market scenarios might map to the same latent point but have different future outcomes (due to hidden variables). This creates "crossing trajectories" that the ODE cannot model.   

    Result: Faced with impossible-to-model crossing trajectories, the Neural ODE averages the vector fields, often resulting in a net zero vector field (f≈0). This is a structural reason for the collapse to the identity function.

3.3 Posterior Collapse in World Models

MuZero learns a Value-Equivalent Model, meaning the latent state is optimized to predict value, not necessarily to reconstruct observations.  

    Information Bypass: When an auxiliary prediction head (like the Neural ODE) is attached, it often suffers from Posterior Collapse, similar to VAEs. The powerful autoregressive decoder (or ODE solver) learns to ignore the conditioning latent state z and simply models the marginal distribution of the output data (prices).   

Consequence: The world model "decouples" from the agent's actions. It predicts price movements based on history but ignores the impact of the agent's BUY/SELL decisions (which should affect portfolio value, if not price). This renders the model useless for planning, as V(s′,Buy)≈V(s′,Hold) in the model's "hallucination".  

4. Coupling: The Vicious Cycle Dynamics

The interaction between Policy Collapse (Section 2) and Prediction Collapse (Section 3) creates a stable equilibrium of failure.
4.1 The Cycle of Stagnation

The collapse proceeds through a identifiable sequence of feedback loops:

    Initialization Phase: The policy is random. The model is untrained.

    The Transaction Cost Filter: The agent executes random BUY/SELL actions. Due to transaction costs (c) and lack of predictive skill, these actions yield negative advantage (A<0). The HOLD action yields A≈0.

    Policy Contraction: The optimizer updates the policy to increase the probability of HOLD. Entropy decreases.

    Data Starvation: The replay buffer fills with trajectories consisting largely of HOLD actions.

    Model Atrophy: The Neural ODE trains on this data. Since the action is always HOLD, the model never learns the causal consequence of trading. Furthermore, minimizing MSE on HOLD trajectories reinforces the identity function (prices move randomly, best prediction is current price).

    Planning Blindness: MCTS uses this atrophied model. When the search tries to expand a BUY node, the model (trained only on HOLD) returns a trivial next state or an inaccurate one. The value function V(s) evaluates this state as no better than the current state.

    Search Collapse: The MCTS values Q(s,a) converge for all actions. The search falls back to the policy prior P(s,a), which is already biased toward HOLD.

    Lock-in: The network neurons responsible for features relevant to active trading become dormant. The system enters a low-capacity state from which it cannot escape.

4.2 Reanalyze: Reinforcing the Collapse

MuZero's Reanalyze mechanism, designed to improve sample efficiency, becomes a liability in this scenario.

    Mechanism: Old trajectories are re-run through the current network to update policy targets.

    Effect: If the current network has collapsed to HOLD, Reanalyze takes older, potentially diverse trajectories and re-labels them with the target HOLD. This effectively "bleaches" the history of exploration, forcing the entire dataset to conform to the collapsed policy.   

5. Mitigation Strategies: Breaking the Cycle

Resolving this coupled collapse requires a multi-faceted intervention strategy targeting the policy, the model, and the training dynamics.
5.1 Restoring Plasticity: The ReDo Algorithm

To counteract the Dormant Neuron Phenomenon, the ReDo (Recycling Dormant Neurons) algorithm is essential.  

    Logic: Continuously monitor neuron activity. When a neuron's activation falls below a threshold (becoming dormant), reset its weights to reintroduce plasticity.

    Implementation:

        Compute the "score" of each neuron i in layer l: Sil​=Ex∼D​[∣ϕil​(x)∣].

        Identify dormant neurons where Sil​<τ (e.g., τ=0.1).

        Reset: Reinitialize the incoming weights Win​ of dormant neurons using the original initialization scheme (e.g., Kaiming Uniform).

        Zero Out: Set the outgoing weights Wout​ to zero. This ensures the reset does not immediately disrupt the network's output, allowing the neuron to "wakes up" gradually via gradient descent.

    Benefit: This prevents the permanent loss of capacity during the early "risk-averse" phase, ensuring the network remains capable of learning complex strategies later.

5.2 Enforcing Dynamics: Flow Matching

To prevent the Neural ODE from learning the trivial identity function, the training objective must be shifted from state matching to velocity matching.  

    Mechanism: Instead of minimizing ∣∣ODESolve(zt​)−zt+1​∣∣2, use Flow Matching. Regress the vector field fθ​(zt​,t) directly to a target velocity field vt​ constructed from the data.

    Conditional Flow Matching (CFM): Define the target path as a linear interpolation between zt​ and zt+1​. The target velocity is vt​=zt+1​−zt​. $$ \mathcal{L}{FM} = \mathbb{E}{t, z_t} \left[ |

| f_\theta(z_t, t) - (z_{t+1} - z_t) ||^2 \right] $$

    Benefit: This objective is simulation-free (stable gradients) and forces the network to learn the direction of change. It cannot satisfy the loss by outputting zero (unless zt+1​=zt​ exactly, which is rare in markets). It avoids the "crossing trajectories" problem by smoothing the vector field probability.   

5.3 Decoupling Representation: Augmented Temporal Contrast (ATC)

To prevent the representation from collapsing due to the policy's stagnation, the representation learning must be decoupled from the RL objective.  

    Technique: Introduce an auxiliary Augmented Temporal Contrast (ATC) loss.

    Implementation:

        Encode observation ot​ into latent zt​.

        Encode future observation ot+k​ into latent zt+k​.

        Apply strong data augmentations (e.g., random masking, noise) to both.

        Contrastive Loss: Train the encoder to maximize the similarity between zt​ and zt+k​ (positive pair) while minimizing similarity to random negatives zj​ from the batch.

    Benefit: The encoder is forced to learn features that distinguish temporal states regardless of value. Even if V(st​)≈V(st+k​), the encoder must map them to distinct points to satisfy the contrastive loss. This ensures the world model has a rich state space to operate on, preventing the "blind search" problem.

5.4 Adaptive Entropy Regularization (AER)

Fixed entropy coefficients fail because the "safe" reward basin is too deep. Adaptive Entropy Regularization (AER) dynamically adjusts the penalty to enforce exploration.  

    Constrained Optimization: Treat entropy as a constraint, not a bonus.
    maximize E[r]subject to H(π)≥Htarget​

    Lagrangian Multiplier: Introduce a learnable coefficient α (log-temperature).
    L(α)=α(H(π)−Htarget​)

    Dynamics: If entropy drops below Htarget​ (e.g., 3.0), α automatically increases, making the penalty for determinism severe. This forces the policy to spread probability mass, breaking the lock on HOLD even if it temporarily reduces reward.   

5.5 Reward Shaping and MCTS Noise

    Differential Reward: To combat the bias towards HOLD, reshape the reward to be relative to a baseline (e.g., Buy-and-Hold or Market Index).
    rt​=ragent​−rbenchmark​

    If the market goes up and the agent HOLDS, it receives a negative reward (opportunity cost). This incentivizes action.   

Dirichlet Noise Scaling: In AlphaZero, noise is added to the root prior: P(s,a)=(1−ϵ)P+ϵη. For trading, the standard ϵ=0.25 is often too low. Increase ϵ (e.g., to 0.5) during the initial phases to force the MCTS to evaluate BUY/SELL branches despite the pessimistic value estimates.

7. Implementation Roadmap

To rectify the dual collapse in the specific MuZero-based trading agent described, the following implementation steps are recommended:

    Phase 1: Architecture Overhaul.

        Integrate ReDo callbacks to monitor and reset dormant neurons in the policy network (every ~1000 updates).

        Replace the Neural ODE integration loss with a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective.

    Phase 2: Decoupled Pre-training.

        Pre-train the Representation and Dynamics networks using Augmented Temporal Contrast (ATC) and Flow Matching on historical data without the policy head. This forces the model to learn market volatility before the policy can bias the data.

    Phase 3: Constrained Policy Learning.

        Initialize the policy with Adaptive Entropy Regularization, setting a hard target entropy (e.g., uniform distribution entropy - 10%).

        Use Differential Sharpe Ratio rewards to penalize missed opportunities (holding during a bull run).

    Phase 4: Stabilized MCTS.

        Increase Dirichlet noise ϵ to 0.5.

        Implement Entropy-Guided Sequence Weighting (EGSW) to prioritize training on trajectories where the agent took risks and succeeded, rather than safe HOLD trajectories.   

By systematically addressing the mathematical instabilities in the ODE solver and the entropy dynamics of the policy, the "vicious cycle" of collapse can be broken, restoring the agent's ability to learn robust trading strategies.
